The 3 pm slump: what
your lunch has to do with it

Nutri Café TeamFood & nutrition
In this article

    It is 2:45 in the afternoon. The lecture slides blur, your eyelids get heavy, and the sentence you just read has to be read again. Almost everyone knows the afternoon slump. What fewer people know is that it has two causes - and only one of them is your lunch.

    A hungry organ

    Your brain makes up about 2% of your body weight but uses roughly 20% of the energy your body burns at rest. Its preferred fuel is glucose, and an adult brain uses something in the region of 120 g of it a day. It does not store much fuel of its own, so it depends on a steady supply from the blood.

    That is why what - and how much - you eat at lunch can change how the next two hours feel.

    Part of it is your body clock

    Researchers have measured a dip in alertness and performance in the early afternoon even in people who skip lunch. It is part of the body's circadian rhythm: a small, natural lull between the morning and evening peaks of alertness. In many cultures, the afternoon nap grew around it.

    So you cannot eat your way out of the dip completely. But a meal can make it noticeably deeper or shallower, and a short night of sleep makes it worse whatever you eat.

    You can't eat your way out of the dip entirely - but your plate decides how deep it goes.

    Part of it is your plate

    Large meals, and meals made mostly of quickly digested carbohydrate, are linked with more sleepiness afterwards. A big plate of white rice with a little curry digests fast, sends blood glucose up quickly and lets it fall again. A mixed meal - with protein, vegetables, some fat and fibre - slows the rate at which the stomach empties, so glucose rises more gently.

    Two practical findings from research are worth knowing:

    • Order matters. In a study of adults with type 2 diabetes, eating the vegetables and protein of a meal before the carbohydrate led to noticeably lower glucose after the meal than eating the same food in reverse order. The effect has mostly been studied in people with diabetes, but the principle is simple to try: start with the shak and the fish.
    • A short walk helps. Light walking after eating - even a few minutes - lowers the rise in blood glucose compared with sitting still. A walk to your next class counts.

    Water and caffeine

    Even mild dehydration has been linked with poorer attention and more fatigue, and Dhaka afternoons make it easy to fall behind on water. Tea and coffee do increase alertness, but caffeine stays in the body for hours - a 4 pm cup can still be affecting your sleep at midnight, which then makes tomorrow's slump worse.

    A slump-friendly campus lunch

    1. Portion the rice. One measured plate (about 200 g cooked) rather than refills.
    2. Add a real protein. Fish, chicken, egg or a thick dal.
    3. Fill a third of the plate with vegetables - and eat them first.
    4. Walk for ten minutes before you sit down again.
    5. Drink a glass of water with the meal, and keep the strong tea for earlier in the day.
